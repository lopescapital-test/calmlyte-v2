森林浴

Bathing in the forest atmosphere

Shinrin yoku translates literally as forest bathing. The term was coined in 1982 by the Japanese Forestry Agency, which encouraged people to spend unhurried time among trees — not to exercise, and not to arrive anywhere.

The practice is simply presence. Attention moves through the senses one at a time: light coming apart in the canopy, the sound of water somewhere below, the smell of soil after rain, the change in temperature when you step into shade. Japan has since designated official forest therapy trails, and the practice has travelled well beyond it.
